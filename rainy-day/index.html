Rainy day in Bangkok

Things to do in Bangkok when it's raining (from someone that actually lives here)

Some cheap, some a treat, some free, and most of them easy to do on your own. Tap "Book" to see times and prices.

Some of the links on this page are affiliate links, which means I may earn a small commission if you book through them, at no extra cost to you.

Book ahead

  1. Muay Thai at Rajadamnern Stadium

    A historic stadium that has been running fights since 1945, and it's a weather-protected venue, so the rain doesn't matter.

    These tickets are non-refundable, so double-check your date.

    via GetYourGuide Book

  2. Thai cooking class

    Cook a few Thai dishes, then sit down and eat them. A class is an easy way to meet people if you're on your own.

    via Viator Book

  3. Japanese onsen

    Hot baths and nowhere to be. A very good way to spend a grey afternoon.

    via Viator Book

  4. Spa day

    Book a proper spa day and let the rain do its thing outside.

    via Klook Book

  5. Cave Fantasy at MBK Center

    Digital art, lights and glasses-free 3D across themed galleries, and great for photos. It's inside MBK, right by the BTS.

    via Klook Book

  6. Jurassic World: The Experience

    Life-sized dinosaurs and movie-inspired zones at Asiatique The Riverfront. Completely silly, and exactly what a wet afternoon needs.

    via Klook Book

  7. Space & Time Cube

    More than 20 immersive light and digital zones, all indoors. It's out at Seacon Bangkae, so save it for a full afternoon.

    via Klook Book

  8. MOCA Bangkok

    The Museum of Contemporary Art: five floors of Thai art. It's north of the centre, so get a Grab.

    via Klook Book

  9. Akara Sky Hanuman

    A Thai cultural rooftop with 360-degree views over Bangkok and a mirrored observatory. There's an air-conditioned sky dome cinema too, plus nightly Thai dance and Muay Thai shows.

    via Klook Book

  10. Head spa

    A scalp massage and hair wash in one, and a lovely way to switch off.

    via Klook Book

  11. Cinema

    A film is the easiest way to spend a few dry hours, and both chains are in most big malls. Book your seat first.

    Major or SF Cinema Major SF Cinema

  12. Escape room at MBK Center

    Grab a friend or a group of new people and get locked in a room. Book a slot before you go.

  13. Airport lounge

    If you're flying out, head to the airport early and check into the airport lounge instead of waiting at the gate.

    via GetYourGuide Book

Free things to do

  • Bangkok Art & Culture Centre (BACC)

    Nine floors of contemporary art, with quirky craft shops, an art library and cafes on the lower floors. A skywalk links it to BTS National Stadium, so you can get there without an umbrella.

    Free to enter. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 8pm. A few special exhibitions may charge.
  • Bangkok Kunsthalle

    A raw, industrial contemporary art space in Chinatown, near Hua Lamphong MRT. Installations built around shadows, echoes and neon, with a small cafe to dry off in.

    Free to enter. Open Wednesday to Sunday, 2pm to 8pm.
  • Bangkokian Museum

    Old wooden heritage houses in Bang Rak showing how Bangkok families lived in the 1940s and 1950s. Peaceful, and it feels like stepping back in time. Dress modestly.

    Free to enter. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 4pm.
  • Talad Noi Museum

    A small community museum in one of Bangkok's oldest neighbourhoods, with historic photos and objects from the area's Thai-Chinese heritage. A good place to duck into while you're wandering Chinatown's narrow alleys.

    Free to enter. Check opening hours before you go.
  • SookSiam at ICONSIAM

    An indoor Thai floating market on the ground floor of the mall. It's free to wander round the pavilions and canals, and only the food costs money.

Shopping malls

  • CentralWorld

    A huge mall on Rama I Road in the middle of the shopping district. Somewhere to wander for hours while it pours.

  • ICONSIAM

    A riverside mall with the SookSiam indoor floating market on the ground floor.

  • Terminal 21

    An easy rainy-day stop on Sukhumvit, right by the BTS.

  • MBK Center

    Cheap shopping and food courts, and it's where Cave Fantasy is if you want to combine them.

Walking around is free. You only pay if you buy something.

Flooding varies a lot by area, so check your route before you head out. The BTS and MRT are usually the easiest ways to get around when it's pouring.
